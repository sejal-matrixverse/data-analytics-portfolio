# Retail Sales Analytics

## Project Objective

Analyze retail order data to uncover sales trends, customer purchasing behavior, regional performance, and product profitability. Build an interactive dashboard that helps business stakeholders make data-driven decisions.

## Business Questions

1. What are the total sales and profit?
2. Which products generate the highest revenue?
3. Which categories are most profitable?
4. Which regions underperform?
5. Which customers contribute the most revenue?
6. How do discounts affect profitability?

## Tools

- PostgreSQL

## Status

 Completed



 "Tell me about your Retail Sales Analytics project."

I developed an end-to-end Retail Sales Analytics project to analyse sales performance, profitability, product performance, and regional and customer segment trends.

I started by importing and validating the retail transaction data in PostgreSQL. I used SQL to perform business KPI analysis, sales and product analysis, customer and regional analysis, and profitability analysis. I also used advanced SQL concepts such as window functions, CTEs, and SQL views to create reusable analytical datasets and calculated metrics such as gross sales, discounts, net sales, total cost, profit, and profit margin.

I then used Power BI to build an interactive four-page dashboard covering Executive Overview, Profitability Analysis, Product Performance, and Regional and Segment Analysis. I created DAX measures and interactive filters to allow users to explore business performance from different perspectives.

The analysis helped identify key sales and profitability drivers, high-performing products and regions, the performance of different customer segments, and products where discounts or costs were impacting profitability.

The main objective of the project was to demonstrate an end-to-end analytics workflow, starting from raw data and SQL analysis through to business intelligence reporting and actionable business insights.


"What was your SQL workflow?"

I followed a structured SQL workflow starting with database and table creation, followed by data import and validation. I then created calculated metrics and performed business KPI, sales, product, customer, regional, and profitability analysis. I used window functions and CTEs for advanced analysis and created reusable SQL views that could serve as a data layer for Power BI.

"What did you use Power BI for?"

I used Power BI to transform the SQL analysis into an interactive business intelligence solution. I created KPI cards, trend analysis, category and regional comparisons, product rankings, profitability analysis, and interactive slicers. The dashboard was structured into four pages so that stakeholders could move from high-level performance to detailed product and regional analysis.

"What were your key business insights?"

Use this structure:

The analysis showed that sales volume alone does not necessarily indicate profitability. Some products and categories generated strong sales but had relatively lower margins. Regional and customer segment performance also varied, highlighting the importance of analysing both sales and profit margins. Discounting was another important factor because higher discounts can reduce profitability even when sales increase.

"Why did you use SQL Views?"


I used SQL Views to create reusable analytical datasets. Instead of repeating complex calculations in multiple queries, I created views containing metrics such as gross sales, discount amount, net sales, total cost, and profit. This provides a cleaner analytical layer that can be consumed by reporting tools such as Power BI.
